### 🖼️ The Lab: A Case Study in Worldbuilding

> *This is the protagonist's laboratory at Kim Il Sung University, Pyongyang. Generated by AI with a deliberately over-engineered prompt.*

![Lab Background](Assets/Resources/bg/lab.jpg)

**At first glance**: A cozy pixel-art lab. Desk, computer, window, warm lighting. Looks like any "academic" scene in a cozy game, right?

**Look closer.**

Every element in this room is a **political and cultural statement**:

| Element | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Portraits of Kim Il-sung & Kim Jong-il** | Mandatory in every North Korean institution. Their presence is the first thing any North Korean viewer would notice. |
| **"Intelligent Dispatch System Research Lab"** in Korean | The protagonist studies railway AI — a cutting-edge field in a country that, in our world, has limited railway infrastructure. |
| **Juche Tower & Ryugyong Hotel** outside the window | The view is unmistakably Pyongyang. The Ryugyong Hotel — the world's tallest unoccupied building — is a symbol of unrealized ambition. |
| **"Chollima" brand computer** | Named after the "Chollima" (千里马) speed campaign, a North Korean ideological movement. A retro-futuristic detail. |
| **Traditional celadon pottery + modern tech** | The tension between Korea's 5000-year history and its 2076 future. The old and the new, side by side. |
| **Korean calendar marking 2076** | The year the world's railways died. The year our story begins. |
| **"일심단결" (Single-hearted Unity) slogan** | A regime slogan, placed on the wall as background detail — as ubiquitous in North Korea as Coca-Cola ads are in the West. |

**The contrast is the point.**

This is a game where:
- The protagonist graduates from **North Korea's top university** — but returns to a **remote Chinese village** to revive a dying railway.
- The lab is **warm, studious, hopeful** — but the world outside has **abandoned railways for sand-powered flying cars**.
- The technology is **retro-futuristic** — advanced enough to build AI, but culturally rooted in a **hermit kingdom's aesthetic**.

The "cozy pixel art" style makes the ideological weight **go down easy**. You don't notice the propaganda posters until the third playthrough. That's the point.

> *This is not a "generic" game. The setting is deliberate. Every pixel is chosen.*

## 🎮 The Game

### Story

> 2076. Sand-powered flying vehicles have rendered railways obsolete. You inherit a abandoned line in the remote village of Wufeng from your grandfather. Restore the railway, win back the community's trust, and fight against the Sand-Tech monopoly.

### Gameplay

- **Hardcore Economy** — Operations lose money at first. Story missions keep you alive. Every decision matters.
- **Slow-Paced Strategy** — The world runs continuously. You observe trends, make long-term choices, face irreversible consequences.
- **Sand-Tech Rival** — An AI-driven competitor that actively campaigns, price-wars, and tries to acquire your lines.
- **Crew Management** — 5 unique characters with skills, fatigue, training, and role assignments.
- **Tech Tree** — 3 mutually exclusive branches (Industrial / Ecological / Automation).
- **Political Cycles** — Local government shifts between Authoritarian, Market, and Welfare orientations.
- **Story-Driven** — Prologue as Visual Novel, then transitions into management gameplay with ongoing narrative events.
- **Random Events** — 10+ event templates: economic shifts, weather, personnel, Sand-Tech actions.

### Key Formulas

- **Daily passenger flow**: `population × 0.001 × (0.5 + trust × 0.005) × (1 - sandPenetration) × (1 + conductorLevel × 0.03)`
- **Daily fuel cost**: `58.5 × 92km / 100 × (1 + (100 - trainCondition) / 200) × 15 sand/升`
- **Accident probability**: `0.5% × ageFactor × driverSkillFactor × maintenanceFactor × weatherFactor`
- **Trust change**: `normalOps(+0.014/day) - accident(0.08×severity) - sandAds(0.03)`
